# Evidence-Based Academic Writing

Read the [academic integrity rules](../../../docs/academic-integrity.md), the project brief, the [manuscript guidance](../../../manuscript/README.md), and relevant evidence. Select the mode: outline, structural revision, argument strengthening, language editing, or export.

## Procedure

1. Define the function of the section in relation to the research questions and contribution. Preserve the author’s intellectual position; justify substantive changes.
2. For major claims, inspect the chain: claim → evidence → reasoning → inferential limit. Do not add citations merely to make a paragraph look academic.
3. Write paragraphs with a clear main idea, relevant support, and argumentative connection. Distinguish facts, interpretations, normative statements, and hypotheses.
4. Preserve consistency of terminology, questions, constructs, units of analysis, design, and contribution across the manuscript. Do not edit style while silently changing method or meaning.
5. Match citations to verified metadata in `references.bib`. Do not claim to have read a source that was accessed only through a secondary source.
6. If evidence is insufficient, qualify or remove unsupported claims and leave a specific open marker when needed. Do not fill a gap with confident language.
7. Check quotations/translations, locators, citation fit, tables/figures, and institutional style requirements. Do not treat text-similarity checking as integrity verification.

## Export and output

Markdown is the canonical source. A human-readable bibliography generated from `references.bib` must not become a second metadata authority. When export tooling exists, check unresolved citations, reference ordering, duplicate bibliography entries, numbering, layout, and source versions. Do not claim a visual check unless the rendered output was actually opened.

Report whether the work changes language only or also changes substance, what evidence was inspected, and what questions remain unresolved. A results section with no data must remain an outline, not a hypothetical narrative written as if findings already existed.

## Language

**Research outputs are written in English by default.** Use another language only when the researcher states it explicitly, or when the institution, outlet, or participants require it, as recorded in `research/project-brief.md`. Do not switch the output language on your own initiative, and do not infer it from the language of the conversation or of the source material.

Source material, transcripts, field notes, and documents stay in their original language. Translate a quotation only in the output, keep the original alongside it where the design or outlet requires, and mark every translation as one. When translating or editing multilingual material, preserve claim strength, uncertainty, technical terminology, quotations, and attribution.

## Place in the research flow

This skill serves Stage 7 with `research-theory` and owns Stage 8 of the [research flow](../../../docs/research-workflow.md). Follow the [writing standards](../../../docs/writing-standards.md).

- No outline before FG3 is READY; no prose before the [manuscript outline](../../../templates/manuscript-outline.md) is complete down to the topic sentence of every paragraph, each paragraph naming the `brain.md` statements it carries. Record the outlet's limits in the outline first: word count, abstract type and limit, title cap, keyword count, reference style.
- Draft in evidence order — method, results, discussion, conclusion, introduction, abstract, title, keywords — so the framing is written against what the study did rather than what it hoped to do.
- The title, abstract, and keywords follow the [title and abstract rules](../../../docs/abstract-and-title.md) and are assembled from registered statements, not written from memory. Where the design has an abstract checklist (PRISMA for Abstracts, CONSORT for Abstracts), it is registered as a parameter at Stage 5 and verified at FG4. Every number in the abstract matches the results and the run that produced it.
- Display items follow the figures-and-tables rules in the [writing standards](../../../docs/writing-standards.md): each earns its place, never duplicates the text, stands alone through its caption, shows uncertainty, and cites the `DAT` or `RUN` behind it.
- The manuscript may only say what the brain holds. A sentence that asserts something with no statement behind it is either registered first or removed.
- Introduction in six moves: phenomenon → urgency, theoretical grounding, main problem, research gap, state of the art and novelty, RQ with implications and the contribution statement. Novelty is shown through gap and contribution, not claimed as "first".
- Discussion in order: principal findings; findings versus theory; dialogue with literature (finding → supports which literature → differs where → why → what this study adds → limit of interpretation); theoretical and practical implications with the actor named. Every implication promised in the introduction is delivered or withdrawn.
- Limitations name the inference they weaken; the conclusion answers the research questions and stops.
- Before FG4: complete the reporting checklist registered at Stage 5 and the abstract checklist where the design has one, audit claim verbs against the boundaries of inference, verify every number in the abstract against the results and its run, check terminology against the outline, and perform a reverse outline.
- Stage 9, revision: record reviewer comments verbatim in the [revision table](../../../templates/revision-table.md), decompose them into `REV` points, state the concern behind each comment and whether that reading is confirmed or inferred, route each point to the layer it hits, and give the reason each change answers the concern. Do not trim a comment into something easier to answer, and do not close a substantive criticism because the wording was polished. A point routed to design or framing reopens its flow gate and returns to that stage. Build the [response letter](../../../templates/review-response.md) from the table after the changes exist. See the [revision rules](../../../docs/revision.md).

## Execution discipline

Apply the cross-skill principles in `research-rigor`: do not silently resolve uncertainty, use the minimum sufficient complexity, keep changes within task scope, and verify outcomes against explicit acceptance criteria. For high-impact claims or decisions, inspect counterevidence and alternative explanations before strengthening the conclusion.
