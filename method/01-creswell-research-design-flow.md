# Creswell Research Design Flow

## Purpose

This document translates Creswell & Creswell's research-design framework into an operational reasoning flow for AI-assisted research. Read it before any recommendation about qualitative, quantitative, or mixed-methods design.

## 1. Start with the research problem

A method is not the starting point.

The AI must first establish:

- What issue, concern, contradiction, deficiency, or real-world problem is being addressed?
- What is already known?
- What is not yet understood?
- What kind of knowledge is required to address the problem?
- Is the study primarily trying to **explore meaning**, **test relationships or effects**, or **combine complementary forms of evidence**?

A request such as "use SEM," "do interviews," or "use mixed methods" is not sufficient methodological justification. Treat it as a proposed technique, not as an approved research design.

## 2. Clarify the purpose or study aim

The study purpose should state what the research intends to accomplish. It narrows the research problem and becomes the bridge to research questions.

The AI should distinguish among intentions such as:

- explore or understand a phenomenon;
- describe a population or phenomenon;
- examine relationships among variables;
- compare groups;
- test an intervention or treatment;
- explain an unexpected quantitative result;
- develop a context-sensitive instrument, model, intervention, or assessment;
- integrate different forms of evidence to achieve a more complete understanding.

Do not move to design selection until the purpose is sufficiently clear.

## 3. Form the research questions

Research questions further narrow the purpose.

### Qualitative tendency

Questions normally emphasize exploration and understanding.

Typical intent:

- What is the meaning of ...?
- How do participants experience ...?
- How does a process unfold ...?
- How is a phenomenon understood in a particular setting?

Avoid forcing predefined variables into an exploratory qualitative question unless the selected design or theoretical framework requires them.

### Quantitative tendency

Questions or hypotheses commonly concern:

- description;
- relationships or associations;
- prediction;
- group comparison;
- intervention or treatment effects.

Variables should be explicit and measurable.

### Mixed-methods tendency

A mixed-methods study should normally contain:

1. a quantitative question or hypothesis;
2. a qualitative question;
3. an integration question explaining what will be learned by combining the two strands.

## 4. Make philosophical assumptions visible

Creswell & Creswell connect research approaches with the philosophical assumptions researchers bring to a study.

Better Research uses four worldview categories as the default vocabulary.

### Postpositivist

Often aligned with quantitative research. Typical commitments include empirical observation, measurement, theory testing, fallible knowledge, bias control, validity, and reliability.

### Constructivist

Often aligned with qualitative research. Typical commitments include multiple participant meanings, social and historical construction, context, interpretation, and inductive development of meaning.

### Transformative

Often aligned with studies concerned with power, inequality, marginalization, collaboration, reform, or change. It may inform qualitative, quantitative, or mixed-methods components when justified.

### Pragmatic

Often aligned with mixed methods. The research problem and intended consequences take priority, and researchers may draw on multiple forms of data and procedures when this provides a better understanding of the problem.

## 5. Select the broad research approach

### Recommend qualitative when

The study needs to explore or understand meaning, experience, context, process, or an insufficiently understood phenomenon.

Qualitative research is especially appropriate when important variables are not yet known, participant perspectives are central, context matters strongly, existing explanations do not adequately fit the group or setting, or the design needs to remain emergent.

### Recommend quantitative when

The study needs to test a theory or explanation, examine relationships among variables, identify predictors of an outcome, compare groups, estimate population trends/attitudes/opinions, or test a treatment/intervention.

### Recommend mixed methods when

Neither quantitative nor qualitative evidence alone is adequate for the research problem and meaningful integration of both can produce additional insight.

Do **not** recommend mixed methods merely because it sounds more comprehensive, the researcher wants "more data," interviews and surveys are both available, or the design appears more sophisticated. There must be a clear rationale for integration.

## 6. Select a design within the approach

```text
RESEARCH APPROACH
│
├── QUALITATIVE
│   ├── Descriptive method
│   ├── Narrative research
│   ├── Phenomenology
│   ├── Grounded theory
│   ├── Ethnography
│   └── Case study
│
├── QUANTITATIVE
│   ├── Survey / nonexperimental family
│   │   ├── Cross-sectional
│   │   └── Longitudinal
│   └── Experimental family
│       ├── True experiment
│       ├── Quasi-experiment
│       └── Single-subject design
│
└── MIXED METHODS
    ├── Core designs
    │   ├── Convergent
    │   ├── Explanatory sequential
    │   └── Exploratory sequential
    └── Complex designs
        ├── Mixed-methods experimental / intervention
        ├── Mixed-methods case study
        ├── Mixed-methods participatory / social justice
        └── Mixed-methods evaluation
```

Creswell & Creswell also acknowledge other viable designs and advanced quantitative techniques. Better Research must not pretend that this taxonomy exhausts research methodology.

## 7. Select specific methods only after the design

Methods are the concrete procedures for sampling, recruitment, data collection, measurement, observation, interviewing, document/digital-material collection, data preparation, analysis, interpretation, validation, and reporting.

The design constrains the method. The method should not be selected first and used to invent a design later.

## 8. Build methodological coherence

Before a method recommendation can be marked ready for researcher approval, test:

```text
Problem
  ↕
Purpose
  ↕
Research questions
  ↕
Worldview
  ↕
Approach
  ↕
Design
  ↕
Sampling
  ↕
Data collection
  ↕
Analysis
  ↕
Interpretation
  ↕
Validity / reliability / integration
```

A mismatch anywhere in the chain requires revision.

Examples:

- causal wording with a nonexperimental design;
- phenomenology when the question is actually about organizational process;
- grounded theory without theory-generation intent;
- mixed methods with two datasets but no integration;
- a survey instrument with no evidence of validity or reliability;
- qualitative work with no reflexivity or validation strategy.

## 9. Researcher decision checkpoint

After evaluating alternatives, the AI must provide:

- recommended approach;
- recommended design;
- methodological rationale;
- worldview alignment;
- alternatives considered;
- reasons alternatives were rejected;
- assumptions;
- unresolved questions;
- additional evidence required;
- specialist methodological sources required;
- decision status.

Recommended workflow states:

- `PROPOSED` — AI recommendation awaiting human judgment.
- `APPROVED` — researcher explicitly accepts the design.
- `REVISION REQUIRED` — methodological mismatch remains.
- `BLOCKED` — insufficient information to make a defensible recommendation.

The AI must not mark its own recommendation `APPROVED`.
