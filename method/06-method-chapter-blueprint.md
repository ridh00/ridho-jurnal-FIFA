# Method Chapter Blueprint

## Purpose

This file provides a practical writing blueprint for the methodology/method chapter after the research design has been reasoned through and approved.

It is an **operational Better Research structure derived from the topics Creswell & Creswell require researchers to address**. It is not presented as a verbatim chapter outline from the book.

Do not draft a complete method chapter while major design fields remain unresolved.

## Universal method chapter logic

A method chapter should allow a reader to understand:

```text
Why this approach?
Why this design?
Who/what is studied?
How is evidence selected?
How is evidence collected?
How is evidence analyzed?
How are interpretations warranted?
How are quality and ethics protected?
What can and cannot be concluded?
```

## Recommended shared structure

### 3.1 Research approach and methodological rationale

Write:

- research approach;
- philosophical worldview where relevant;
- relationship between research problem, purpose, and approach;
- rationale for rejecting major alternatives.

Do not write generic textbook definitions for several pages. Justify the actual study.

### 3.2 Research design

Write:

- exact design name;
- what the design means operationally in this study;
- why it fits the research questions;
- unit of analysis / central phenomenon / variables / intervention / integration logic as applicable.

### 3.3 Research setting, context, population, or case

Specify the research site, organizational/social context, target population, participant group, bounded case, cultural group, phenomenon, or intervention context as required by the design.

### 3.4 Sampling and recruitment

Specify:

- sampling logic;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria;
- sampling frame if relevant;
- sample-size logic;
- recruitment;
- access and gatekeepers;
- power analysis or qualitative stopping logic where relevant.

### 3.5 Data sources, instruments, and materials

Specify instruments, interviews, observations, documents, records, digital data, experimental materials, measurement validity/reliability, protocols, and permissions/licensing as applicable.

### 3.6 Data collection procedures

Write the procedure in chronological order. A competent reader should be able to understand what the researcher will actually do.

### 3.7 Data analysis

Map each research question to the analysis used to answer it.

For every analysis explain input data, preparation, procedure, assumptions where relevant, output, and interpretation.

### 3.8 Research quality

#### Quantitative

- validity of measures;
- reliability;
- bias control;
- internal/external validity where applicable;
- statistical assumptions.

#### Qualitative

- reflexivity;
- validation strategies;
- consistency/reliability of procedures;
- evidence for themes and interpretations.

#### Mixed methods

- quality of quantitative strand;
- quality of qualitative strand;
- integrity of integration;
- joint display;
- metainference quality.

### 3.9 Ethics and data management

Address consent, confidentiality, permissions, participant risk, sensitive data, storage, access, retention, reporting, and researcher relationships where relevant.

### 3.10 Methodological limitations and boundaries of inference

State what the design cannot establish, sampling/measurement/context limitations, transferability/generalization limits, unresolved validity threats, and integration limitations if mixed methods.

This prevents the discussion chapter from making claims stronger than the design permits.

## Qualitative chapter variant

```text
Research approach and worldview
Qualitative design
Researcher role and reflexivity
Setting / case / phenomenon
Participants and purposeful sampling
Recruitment and access
Data sources
Interview / observation / document protocols
Data collection
Data preparation
Coding
Theme / description development
Design-specific analytic framework
Interpretation
Validation strategies
Reliability / consistency
Ethics and data management
Limitations
```

The selected qualitative tradition must shape the procedure. Do not write a generic thematic-analysis section and then attach a design label with no operational consequence.

## Quantitative chapter variant

```text
Research approach and worldview
Quantitative design
Population
Sampling frame
Sampling design and sample size
Power analysis where applicable
Variables
Instrumentation and measures
Validity and reliability of scores
Pilot testing
Data collection / survey administration / experiment procedure
Data preparation
Analysis plan
Statistical assumptions
Threats to validity
Preregistration where applicable
Ethics and data management
Boundaries of inference
```

For experiments, additionally address participants, recruitment, random assignment or the reason randomization is unavailable, experimental and control conditions, manipulation, materials, procedure, outcome measures, and internal/external validity threats.

## Mixed-methods chapter variant

```text
Mixed-methods rationale
Worldview
Design name
Design diagram
Quantitative research questions/hypotheses
Qualitative research questions
Mixed-methods integration question

Phase/strand 1
  sampling
  data collection
  analysis

Phase/strand 2
  sampling
  data collection
  analysis

Integration
  intent
  point of integration
  procedure: merge/connect/embed
  joint display
  metainference

Validity of quantitative strand
Validation of qualitative strand
Integration quality / validity
Ethics and data management
Resources / timing / team
Limitations
```

The ordering of phases must match the selected design.

## Drafting rule

Before writing any subsection, identify:

```yaml
subsection:
source_of_decision:
research_question_supported:
facts_available:
assumptions:
evidence_required:
unresolved_fields:
allowed_claim_strength:
```

If critical fields remain unresolved, write a structured placeholder rather than inventing content.

## Traceability rule

Every substantive method statement should trace back to at least one of:

- research question;
- approved design decision;
- verified instrument/source;
- sampling decision;
- ethics decision;
- analysis plan;
- methodological reference.

The method chapter is not the place to hide decisions that were never actually made.
