# Methodological Source Basis

## Primary source

Creswell, J. W., & Creswell, J. D. (2023). *Research Design: Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed Methods Approaches* (6th ed.). SAGE.

This folder primarily operationalizes:

- Chapter 1 — selection of a research approach;
- Chapter 2 — relationship between literature and research approach;
- Chapter 3 — use of theory;
- Chapter 4 — writing and ethical considerations;
- Chapter 5 — research problem and introduction;
- Chapter 6 — purpose statements;
- Chapter 7 — research questions and hypotheses;
- Chapter 8 — quantitative methods;
- Chapter 9 — qualitative methods;
- Chapter 10 — mixed-methods procedures.

## Scope boundary

Creswell & Creswell position the book as a research-design text that highlights essential design features rather than a complete specialist manual for every method.

Better Research therefore uses this source for:

- design architecture;
- approach selection;
- worldview/design/method alignment;
- qualitative/quantitative/mixed-methods distinctions;
- core design families;
- method-section planning;
- methodological coherence.

Specialist references should be added for technical procedures that require deeper treatment.

The copyrighted source book itself is **not** included in this repository.

## Interpretation policy

The documents in this folder are **operational syntheses**, not reproductions of the book.

They should:

- preserve Creswell & Creswell's conceptual distinctions;
- avoid presenting Better Research additions as direct quotations or rules written by Creswell;
- distinguish the book's design framework from Better Research workflow conventions;
- use additional authorities when technical depth is needed.

## Better Research additions

The following are workflow conventions added by Better Research rather than Creswell terminology:

- `PROPOSED / APPROVED / REVISION REQUIRED / BLOCKED`;
- AI recommendation contracts;
- explicit human-approval checkpoints;
- YAML method-input contracts;
- minimum-sufficient-complexity rule;
- Git/Issue/PR traceability;
- epistemic-state labels.

These conventions make methodological decisions inspectable and recoverable across AI sessions.
